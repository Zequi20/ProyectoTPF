  {\Large\bfseries ABSTRACT}

\vspace{0.8\baselineskip}

\begin{spacing}{1.3}

    \justifying
    \setlength{\parskip}{6pt plus 1pt minus 1pt}

    This study aims to evaluate four pixel-to-millimetre conversion procedures for measuring fish with a single camera through the glass of a tank, and to quantify the length interval introduced by an unknown depth. Through the glass, refraction makes the apparent size of the fish depend on its distance from the glass, which a single image cannot reveal. On the same anatomical points, the scale of an ArUco marker attached to the glass (A), a homography from a coplanar marker (\Aprime), an underwater pinhole calibration (B) and a flat-port refractive model (C) were compared. Six rigid replicas, measured with a calliper, were photographed at four distances from the glass, with three captures per condition (72 images). With known depth, B and C obtained the lowest total-length mean absolute errors (MAE), 1.08 mm (0.73\,\%) and 1.22 mm (0.88\,\%), followed by \Aprime{} (3.29 mm) and A (37.43 mm); no difference between B and C was detected (sign test, $p = 1$). Without depth information, the interval of C spanned 56 to 82\,\% of the length and contained the calliper length in all 72 captures; with depth bounded to $\pm 1$~cm, it narrowed to 4--5\,\%, with a coverage of 58 of 72. With known depth, B and C keep the mean absolute percentage error of total length below 1\,\%; without it, depth is the dominant source of uncertainty.
